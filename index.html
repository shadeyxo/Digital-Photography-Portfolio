My name is Vishal Lakshmanan, and I am a senior at Mission San Jose High School. I recently decided to take Digital Photography 1 with MVROP at Mission this year, and I have learned a lot about photography and plan to pursue it as a hobby in the future.

I am most interested in portrait photography because I want to depict the unique stories everyone has just through a picture of their face. I have also played badminton for the school team in both my sophomore and junior years, and it taught me a lot about discipline and grit.

Those values really shaped the goals behind my photography projects and represent what I tried to capture through each of them. I hope my work can inspire future generations to get into this amazing field.

01

Project One

Car Show at
Washington High School

For this project, I decided to shoot a local car meet hosted right in a high school parking lot. The whole theme centered around car culture through the lens of young, everyday enthusiasts. Instead of going after immaculate showroom supercars, I wanted to focus on the raw, real energy of student builds and passion projects.


I captured everything from beat-up projects being built on a budget to freshly polished rides that people poured their weekends into. The bright afternoon sun hitting the asphalt gave the shots a clean, gritty look that fit the high school backdrop perfectly. Looking back at the final images, you can really feel the sense of community and shared pride everyone had in their cars. It was a cool way to show how a simple parking lot can turn into a massive hub for creativity and car lovers.

02

Project Two

Badminton at
Bintang Court, Fremont

For this part of my photo essay, I focused on the badminton scene at the local Bintang court here in Fremont. The main theme was all about showcasing the deep sense of community and connection that thrives inside the gym.


I wanted to capture the everyday moments that make the space feel like a second home, things like the intense rallies, the players laughing and catching their breath on the sidelines, and all the in-between moments people never really see. It was important to me to represent the diverse mix of people who gather there, from young kids picking up a racket for the first time to older players who have been coming for years. The bright overhead lights and vibrant green mats gave the photos a really energetic feel. By the end, the project became less about the sport itself and more about how a local gym can bring a whole community together.

03

Project Three

Portraits &
Player Voices

For the final part of my project, I transitioned into portrait work to create a dedicated feature on individual badminton players. The main theme was to capture the personal identity behind the athlete, giving each player a voice by pairing their portrait with a meaningful quote.


Instead of action shots, I focused on tight, expressive close-ups right after their matches when they were still riding the energy of the game. The quotes they shared ranged from deep reflections on mental toughness to lighthearted jokes about their favorite court rivalries. Pairing their expressions with their own words really highlighted the different personalities and mindsets that make up our local sports community. It ended up being a really personal way to show that behind every jersey and racket, there is a story and a real passion driving them forward.
